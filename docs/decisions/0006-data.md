# 0006 — Public CC BY data, split by site, pinned by hash

## Context

The models are shipped inside the software, so the data they are trained on
must allow redistribution of derived works by anyone, including commercial
users. The evaluation must measure generalisation to data the models have
never seen — and anyone must be able to reproduce it.

## Decision

- Only public collections of The Cancer Imaging Archive under Creative
  Commons Attribution licences (3.0 or 4.0) are used; non-commercial
  collections are excluded. Attribution is in [NOTICE](../../NOTICE).
- Training and test collections are disjoint: different collections come
  from different sites, scanners and protocols, so the test measures
  generalisation across sites, not across patients of one site.
- Series are selected deterministically (ordered by the SHA-256 of their UID,
  one per patient) and pinned in `lab/manifests/tz1.json` with the SHA-256
  of their samples; `tomoz-lab fetch` refuses data that does not match.
  Series that turn out not to hold usable images are marked `excluded` with
  the reason, not silently replaced.
- Training uses windows of 40 central slices per volume and a bounded number
  of samples per slice (proportional to the slice area, so a 13-megapixel
  mammogram counts for more than a 512 × 512 slice). The 2-D model is trained
  on radiographs and mammograms plus four slices of each volume, matching
  what it codes in practice: 2-D images and the first slice of each tile.

## Consequences

- The released models can be used in closed products, with attribution.
- Results depend only on public data and open code; `tomoz-lab fetch` and
  `tomoz-lab eval` reproduce them.
- TCIA may remove or change collections; the hashes detect it, and the
  manifest then needs a new version rather than a silent substitution.
